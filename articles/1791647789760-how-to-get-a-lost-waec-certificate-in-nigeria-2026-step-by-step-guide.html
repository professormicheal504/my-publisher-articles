    Have you lost your WAEC certificate and don't know how to get it back? Maybe you had moved houses recently and it was misplaced in the shuffle, or it was destroyed by water or fire, or stolen, or lost during a move from one place to another.

    The good news is, there's a way to get a digital copy of your certificate or at least an attestation that you passed your WAEC exams. In 2026, WAEC offers digital certificate services from their WAEC Digital Certificate Platform. However, please note, WAEC typically does not issue a new copy of a lost certificate, but you may be eligible for a digital copy of your original, if available, or an attestation of your results, wherever available.

    Here's the step-by-step process to get your lost WAEC certificate in Nigeria.


     Step 1: go to the official WAEC site


    The first thing you'll want to do is go to the official WAEC Digital Certificate Platform. You can do this by heading to

    https://portal.waec.org

    The website allows candidates to download their original WAEC's certificate, wherever available, and recover lost or forgotten examination numbers, as well as other useful information.

    Make sure you visit the official website in order to protect any personal information you may have.

    Step 2: create an account


    If you're visiting the website for the first time, you'll need to create an account.

    To begin, open the official website for WAEC Digital Certificate Service

    Click on the create account option

    and select Certificate Access (Candidate), as you're looking to get your own certificate.

    Once you've done that, fill in the information requested on the page, and follow the instructions to complete your account registration. It's a good idea to save any login information somewhere safe, so you can always log in to the site and access your certificate.

    If you already have an account, skip ahead to the next step.

    Step 3: recover your candidate number (if forgotten)


    Your examination number or candidate number is an essential piece of information to have ready when trying to access your certificate. If you've forgotten it, don't worry, the new WAEC Digital Certificate Services in 2026 also make it easy to recover your lost exam or candidate number.

    Before you do anything else, check to see if you have any old printouts of your WAEC results, examination slips, saved copies of result checking details, or any other information that may have your examination number written down or saved on it.

    If you can't find it, check to see if the official digital certificate site has a recovery option, for your particular examination details, before contacting WAEC directly or visiting their office. If you're able to recover your candidate number on the website, then you won't have to waste time contacting WAEC or waiting for a response from them.

    When contacting WAEC, be prepared to provide your full name, year of examination, type of examination, and any other identifying information they may ask you for.

    Make sure you provide the correct information, to help avoid any unnecessary delays.

    Step 4: access your certificate


    Once you've created your account and recovered your candidate number, it's time to log in to the WAEC Digital Certificate website again, and follow the instructions on your dashboard to locate your certificate.

    You may be able to view an online version of your certificate, or download a copy, wherever available, depending on your examination record and the services available for it.

    If your certificate isn't appearing, double check that the examination information you've provided is correct. Some older examination records may require additional help from WAEC, or just need to be found first!

    Please note that if you're looking to get a replacement for a lost physical certificate, downloading a digital copy of it is not exactly the same as getting a newly printed original one. According to WAEC's official instructions, they typically issue an attestation of results for lost or damaged certificates, rather than a replacement of the original.
