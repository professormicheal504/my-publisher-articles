Stop Reading Everything: The JAMB Study Hack Nobody Told You About

You've got the textbooks. You've got the past questions. You've got 47 tabs open with "how to score 300 in JAMB." And somehow you still feel like you're drowning.

Here's the truth: most JAMB candidates don't fail because they didn't read enough. They fail because they read the wrong things, the wrong way. Here's what to actually start doing today.

1. Kill the syllabus guesswork

JAMB doesn't test "everything you know" — it tests specific, published topics. If you're reading random textbook chapters without checking them against the official syllabus first, you're studying blind. Get the syllabus, tick off topics as you master them, and stop wasting hours on things that won't even show up.

2. Past questions are not revision — they're strategy

Don't just "do" past questions the night before. Start now, and start tracking patterns: which topics repeat every year, which question styles JAMB loves, where you consistently lose marks. Ten years of past questions will teach you more about what's coming than any textbook chapter.

3. Watch it before you read it

If a topic isn't clicking on the page, stop forcing it. A 15-minute video explanation of Number Bases or Mole Concept will often save you an hour of confused reading. Use video lessons as your first pass, then read to lock it in — not the other way around.

4. The compulsory novel is not optional

If you're offering Use of English, your set novel carries real marks every single year. Students skip it thinking "it's just a story" — then lose easy points they could've banked with two evenings of proper reading.

5. Study in 90-minute sprints, not all-day marathons

Your brain stops absorbing new material after roughly 90 minutes of focused work. Long, unbroken "reading all day" sessions feel productive but leak information out the other end. Sprint, break, repeat — you'll retain more in 3 hours than in 8.

6. Score yourself weekly, not just at the end

Take a timed mock CBT every week, from week one — not just in the final month. Early mocks aren't about the score; they're about finding your weak spots while you still have time to fix them.

The real secret? Consistency beats cramming, every single time. Start small today — one topic, one past-question set, one video — and let it compound. Your future 300-scoring self will thank you.
