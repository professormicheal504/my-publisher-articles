The 'NEPA/PHCN Contingency Plan': How I Studied for University Finals During Heavy Blackouts

Believe · 12 October 2026

On this page

    It is 2 am. The air in Ozoro, Nigeria is motionless in a stagnant heat. There is no swish of overhead fans, no humming of transformers, and, most importantly, no light. The only glow in my room comes from the dying light on my smartphone screen and a borrowed rechargeable lamp with a sickly yellow shade.

    This is not an unusual occurrence in Ozoro, especially not for an undergraduate student at Southern Delta University (SDU). However, as a 300-level Software Engineering student facing my first semester final examinations, a total blackout across the entire campus was the last thing I needed.
    For our last set of examinations, the grid simply died. It was a full three-week blackout, stretching right from the end of our revision time to the last day of our examinations.
    Since I am a student of Software Engineering, almost everything I do academically revolves around the electric grid. Never was it easier to lose everything I worked for and fall into despair like my Nigerian university peers.
    The only difference was that I refused to allow PHCN to dictate my Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA).
    By viewing the blackout as a system, I came up with solutions that ended up leveling me on a 4.30 GPA and saving me from the doom of the Second Class upper division.

     Below are the exact strategies I employed during the three-week blackout and how you can use them the next time your campus goes dark.  



     1. The Energy Architecture: Power Management During Blackouts

    When studying for exams during a blackout, the first concern is always energy. Without power, you cannot access your notes, past questions, or even the department’s slide repository.
    I separated my energy ecosystem into three main pillars:

    The Daily Power Bank Rotation

    I had two heavy-duty power banks (20000mAh and 30000mAh). The first one was in constant use to power my smartphone through reading PDFs while the second one served as a backup. It was a strict personal rule that I would not use any power banks to access social media or other data-heavy applications.

    The Charge Center Economy

    When you are in Ozoro but studying for exams away from campus, local business charge centers are your best friends.
    I budgeted for the costs of charging my power banks alongside my weekly feeding budget. I would visit a kiosk that had a diesel generator and charge my power banks every morning at 7 am. The fee for a charge was between ₦100 and ₦200, depending on the size of the power bank.
    The rule was that I should never have both power banks at less than fifty percent.

    The Solar Alliance

    It never hurts to have friends and family. One of my flatmates who studied off-campus had a small solar inverter. Whenever the local kiosks had a long queue or the generator had run out of fuel, I could visit him for a few hours. We would review Software Architecture concepts together while I charged my smartphone from his laptop.

    2. The Data Hack: MTN Network Monetization

    Having power is of little use if you do not have data. As a student, it is too expensive to buy top-up data bundles during an exam period. And honestly, heavy data usage can drain your smartphone battery in record time.
    I had to implement what I call the MTN Network Monetization Strategy.
    Instead of buying the expensive MTN network bundles, I would constantly browse through MTN’s sub-code to see if there were any data offers that interested me. Primarily, I would dial *312*87# to get the ₦50 for 450MB network offer that had a fourteen-day validity period.

    MTN Data Hack Blueprint:
    Dial *312*87# ➔ Deduct ₦50 ➔ Receive 450MB (14 Days Validity)

    While 450MB may not seem like much data, I implemented what I call the Offline-first Protocol.

    Batch Downloading: I only turned on my cellular data for ten minutes at a time. Within that short period, I would go to our department’s WhatsApp groups, Google Drive, and Telegram channels. From there, I would download any relevant material such as textbook PDFs, past questions, or lecture notes.
    Immediate Disconnection: As soon as I had finished downloading, I would turn off my cellular data.
    Re-purchase Strategy: If the 450MB ran out while downloading, I would merely dial the code again. It was cheaper to spend ₦100 to ₦200 a week on roughly a gigabyte of heavily targeted examination material than it was to buy a standard data bundle.

    3. The 'Batch-Processing' Study Timetable

    Studying during a blackout requires that you reorient your study schedule to fit in with your energy resources. I mean, there is no reason you should be studying at night if it is too dark.
    The following is my study timetable during the blackout period:

    Time Slot Activity Energy Status Strategic Objective
    08:00 AM – 12:00 PM Desktop Research &amp; Group Study High Device Charge Discuss complex algorithms, code structures, and system design models with course mates on campus
    12:30 PM – 04:00 PM Device Rest &amp; Manual Summarization Off-Grid / Charging Write brief summary cards, definitions, and database schemas by hand using physical notebooks
    04:30 PM – 07:30 PM Intensive Offline PDF Reading Medium Device Charge Read downloaded materials on the phone screen with the lowest percentage of brightness
    09:00 PM – 12:00 AM Active Recall &amp; Flashcard Drilling Low Device Charge / Lantern Test memory using physical note cards under the rechargeable light. No screen time allowed
    By dividing my day into these slots, I ensured that I did not fully drain my smartphone battery by midday. It also gave my eyes a break from the phone screen by forcing me to read physical note cards.

    4. Adapting to Software Engineering Without a Screen

    Studying abstract concepts such as software design patterns, system architecture, or source code without the aid of a computer screen feels like learning to swim on dry land.
    To pass the examinations of the Department of Computer Science and Engineering while studying for exams in the dark, I had to adapt my learning strategies.

    Whiteboarding Code on Paper

    Since I could not use an Integrated Development Environment (IDE) or write C++ or Java code on a laptop, I had to become intimately acquainted with my notebook. I wrote out entire programs by hand and physically drew out the memory execution blocks.
    By studying this way, I forced my brain to memorize programming concepts such as syntax, memory addresses, and variables. In the end, if the examiner asked me to write out algorithms on paper, it felt a lot like home.

    The Concept Mapping Technique

    When it came to more theoretically inclined courses like Software Quality Assurance and Database Management Systems (DBMS), I drew out entire concept maps. I studied Normalization (1NF, 2NF, 3NF) using a series of relational database models. Being able to create a visual representation of the concepts helped me memorize them with ease.
    Since I could visualize the material, most of the studying I did during the day transferred to what I could do in the dark.

    5. The Critical Psychological Shift

    Studying during a blackout requires that you make a paradigm shift.
    When I first learned that the entire campus would be without power for three weeks, the worst outcome I could imagine was sitting in my dormitory, complaining about the injustice of it all while cursing the university administration and the Nigerian electric grid.
    I had to flip the script and view the situation from a different perspective. I told myself that if I could master system design parameters by kerosene lamp or cheap battery in Ozoro, nothing the world threw at me later would matter.


    Summary Checklist for Nigerian Campus Blackouts
    If you find yourself in the same situation I was in, here is a list of what you should do:

    Secure Two Power Sources: Always keep two power banks, one of which is strictly for your smartphone so that you have access to notes and past papers.
    Audit Local Kiosks: Find a commercial charge center where you can charge your power banks first thing in the morning before the queues build up.
    Minimize Data Costs: Use targeted phone codes to get the best data offers such as the MTN ₦50 for 450MB deal (dial *312*87#)
    Go Offline-First: Turn your smartphone into a portable hard disk by downloading material in short bursts and reading it without cellular data.
    Transcribe to Paper: Always write out digital material onto paper while you have light so that you can study it in the dark.

    The Nigerian educational terrain has enough challenges and pitfalls to waste the academic potential of a brilliant young mind. Earning a 4.30 GPA while an entire campus was in darkness proved to me that, sometimes, a perfect score is achieved not by having the perfect system, but by having the perfect strategy.
