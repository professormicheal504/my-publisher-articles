    Before getting started, please remember this.

     

    Why you need to get your subject combination right before you show off your scores

     

    UTME scores and subject combinations are different things. The score means 'this candidate is intelligent', and the combo is 'this candidate studied for the right course'. It won't work for you to just have high scores for courses that have no relation to medicine and get into  a university. If there are mismatches, your application will be cut off before anyone looks at your scores.

     

    JAMB states the requirements of courses in their brochure. The CAPS admission process uses your registered subjects. Almost always, if there is a small difference, the school will not look over it. And... appeals are hardly simple.

     

    The requirements of the course for each school are in the JAMB brochure.

    How come? It's all about the registration. People are young. People forget when the time is not there yet. And advice comes from anyone. Maybe a cousin who was studying something related. A friend who did this last year. Or perhaps the centre guy managing 40 people before the place closes. Everyone's got the best interests of the candidates. But too many are relying on memory. Or rules from the previous year.

    Another thing is that Many candidates think that, being a science, art or commerce student, they can do any combination of their "trade". Wrong. Same-named courses require different subjects. For instance, you may find that for a particular university, Computer Science takes Biology as the fourth subject. While Computer Science needs your Chemistry. Agricultural Science and Agricultural Engineering may both start with Agricultural. But they're different.

    Four.

     

    It's four subjects in UTME. You must take Use of English for all courses. Even if you are doing an XYZ course. Its 100 marks. Just like the other papers. Don't think it is a formality. Take the time to study it. The other 3 papers are course-based. And these ones, you better not make a blunder.

    Faculty placement is a game changer.

    Most people are not aware of this. Some courses can fall under different faculties in different universities. And that affects the subject combination. For example, Economics may be in Social Science and require you to study Math and Economics in most places. However, if it's under Management Sciences, then the combination may be a bit different. Or, if it's Computer Science and you're studying it under the Faculty of Science, you can use Biology. If under Physical Science, then you'd rather go for Chemistry most times. And if you're studying it in Engineering, then Chemistry is compulsory.

    That's why if you search for "JAMB combination for Economics", you might not get the right thing. You might get the right combination for a particular school but not the school you want to study it in. So you'll need to know in which faculty your course is being offered in each university you are interested in.

    3 Mistakes that cost people 1 year

     

    1. Mixing O-level and Jamb requirements.

     WAEC/Neco needs 5 credit (including English, Math)

     UTME need 4 subject (you choose based on your course)

     A1 in Agriculture in  O Level does not mean it's valid for jamb subject (engineering), for example.

     so check each

     

    2. dodging math

    Some commerce students will do: English, Econ, commerce, Finance, accounting.

    For 2 reasons: to avoid math (thinking to avoid low mark)


     

    3. Missing school specific.

     

    Most Nigerian Universities, not all of them. Some accept Agricultural Science in place of Biology or not. Some technical universities even have their own unique combo for Computer Science. Check the JAMB brochure . Damn, it's long and hard to understand, but chill and take it slow.

     

    Go check all schools. 280 and correct combo is better than 320 and wrong combo.

    Checking combo.

     

    Decoding JAMB BROCHURE

     

    First time checking JAMB brochure? It's confusing.

     

    They list by institution. Then course.

     

    It separates between UTME subjects and Olevel/Direct Entry requirements.

     

    Don't mix them up.

    READ CAREFULLY.

    "Any two of" means it's your choice.

    "And" means you need all subjects listed.

    Sometimes there is a main choice and alternative. Read to the end.

     

    Make sure you know the year.

    Things change.

    A screenshot from last year may not be up to date.

     

    Simple 5 steps checklist

     

    Before you seal the deal, follow this checklist.

    1. Write down the exact name of your course. Don't just write Engineering. Write it like your school spells it.

    2. List your schools.

    Check out the faculty housing your course for each school.

    3. Look at your JAMB brochure.

    Find your course for each of your schools.

    4. Compare with the university website.

    Write down if brochure and university site don't match.

    5. Ask at admissions office.

    If you are still confused, ask them. Request a written answer.

     

    Just a few hours of proper checks and you have avoided wasting an entire year.

     

    If you registered the wrong combo

    Do not panic.

    DO NOT IGNORE.

    Your choice is usually one of these.

    1. Correct your subjects.

    JAMB has a correction process.

    It varies. Dates and rules change.

    See the guidelines in the portal. Ask at an accredited centre. Act once you see the mistake.

    2. Change your course

    If you can't change the subject, find a course that relates to your registered course; it is not exactly what you expected but still makes your score useful.

    3. Explore related courses.

    A combo can be suitable for multiple programmes. Messed up physics for math. Maybe that combo can be used to open doors in Biochemistry or Microbiology in some schools. Check the brocho all the time.

    Anyway, do something before the exams. It's much harder to do something after the exam.

    The Last word
